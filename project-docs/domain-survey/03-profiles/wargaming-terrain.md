# Wargaming Terrain & MDF Kits

**Tier:** physical-output · **Rubric:** Pop 3 · Pain 3 · Fit 4 · GapCost 3 · Adopters 3 = **324** · Longlist A8

## Snapshot
Laser-cut MDF buildings and scenery are a thriving seller economy (kits *and*
cut-files) with flat-pack's exact mechanics — tab-slot walls, etched detail
layers — plus wargaming's own ecosystem of bases, movement trays, and tokens.

## Description
Terrain makers and small manufacturers (Brutal Cities, Uncertain Scenery, TJH
Models tier) producing 28–32 mm scale buildings, ruins, and scatter for
Warhammer/Kill Team/historical play, sold assembled, as kits, or as cut files
on Etsy. Adjacent staples: bases (ovals/rounds at standard sizes), movement
trays, acrylic tokens. Design in Illustrator/Fusion; heavy etch layers for
brick/panel detail.

## Problems Pathogen could address
A building kit is flat-pack + surface detail: tab-slot walls (material-
thickness math), etched brick/window patterns (procedural texture — noise and
Grid territory), and scale variants (28 mm vs 32 mm vs 15 mm re-issues are
manual redraws today). Base/tray generators are pure parameter tables
(standard sizes × magnet holes × slot counts). Tournament "competitive
layout" sets need footprint-consistent families.

## Commercial value
Active kit + cut-file market (Etsy MDF terrain files sell as digital goods);
tournament/event bulk orders; base-and-token accessories are high-volume
consumables. Sellers are exactly the parametric-minded wedge.

## Missing features
### Domain-specific [D]
- Tab-slot wall joints by material thickness (shared with A1 flat-pack)
- Procedural etch textures (brick courses, planking, panel lines) as fills
- Scale-variant regeneration (one building, three scales, thickness re-solved)
- Base/tray/token generators at gaming-standard sizes with magnet holes
### General [G]
- Physical units; sheet nesting; DXF/lightburn export profiles; modules
  (architectural detail libraries)

## Project ideas (as of 2026-10-07)
Tiered by Pathogen skill and readiness. Web-researched; a dated snapshot.

### Beginner — buildable today
- **Objective markers and tokens** — Round objective markers and status tokens
  are high-volume consumables cut from acrylic or MDF. Each is a disc with an
  etched symbol and ring, so the file is a cut layer and an etch layer, and
  Grid lays out a full set on one sheet. Size is set on import into the laser
  software, which keeps it clear of the units gap.
- **Cobblestone base toppers** — Etched toppers glued onto plain bases give an
  army a matching street or ruin theme, and textured bases are sold in many
  sizes. A Grid of stones jittered with noise gives a pavement nobody
  hand-drew, on an etch layer inside a cut outline. Deterministic noise means
  the same seed always reproduces the same topper for a reorder.
- **Brick-etched ruin panels** — A flat wall fragment with etched brick
  courses and a broken top edge is the simplest scenery to cut, glued upright
  onto a strip. Grid lays the courses and noise knocks bricks out and roughens
  the outline; cut and etch stay on separate layers. This is the procedural
  detail competitors draw by hand, and it needs no joints.

### Intermediate — several features, or one gap
- **Movement trays** — Movement trays hold a unit of round-based models in
  formation and are among the most-sold MDF accessories, offered in many
  footprints. A tray is a top layer with a Grid of base pockets over a solid
  bottom layer. The single gap is physical units: pockets must fit 25 mm or 32
  mm bases with a known clearance, not an eyeballed one.
- **Free-standing ruin set** — A starter terrain set is a dozen or more
  pieces: corner ruins, broken walls, barricades. Drawing them as one family,
  with shared brick etch from noise and Grid and labelled cut and etch layers,
  keeps the set visually consistent. The one gap is sheet nesting, since a
  dozen irregular parts still have to be packed onto MDF sheets by hand.
- **Window and door detail sheet** — Kits get their character from overlay
  parts: window frames, doors, buttresses, glued onto plain walls in a second
  layer. A sheet of these is repeated shapes on a Grid with cut and etch
  layers. The gap is modules: architectural details should be a shared library
  that every building file draws from, not shapes copied between files.

### Advanced — depends on a named gap
- **Magnetised bases and conversion trays** — Clubs buy round and oval bases
  with magnet recesses, and conversion trays that let round-based models rank
  up on a square footprint. Every part is a row in a size table. This depends
  on base, tray and token generators at gaming-standard sizes with magnet
  holes, the domain gap that would turn that table into a complete catalog of
  cut files.
- **Shipping containers and crates** — Sci-fi shipping containers and crates
  are standard scatter terrain: closed boxes whose appeal is surface detail,
  corrugation, panel lines and planking. The box is ordinary flat-pack; the
  detail is the product. This depends on procedural etch textures as fills,
  the domain gap that would apply panel lines or planking to any wall shape
  instead of a hand-drawn pattern.
- **Sellable cut-file pack** — Makers now sell the files themselves, tested on
  a stated thickness of MDF, alongside physical kits. Buyers expect files that
  open in their laser software with cut and etch already assigned. This
  depends on DXF and LightBurn export profiles, the general gap that would
  carry Pathogen's layers into the formats and colour conventions a paying
  customer's machine reads.

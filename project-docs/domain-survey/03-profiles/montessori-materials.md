# Montessori & Classroom Materials

**Tier:** physical-output · **Rubric:** Pop 2 · Pain 3 · Fit 4 · GapCost 3 · Adopters 2 = **144** · Longlist D5

## Snapshot
The Montessori-maker convergence is already happening — free laser-file
libraries carry dozens of Montessori toy templates and Etsy sells laser-cut
materials — but the materials' defining trait, dimensional exactness by
pedagogical spec, is served by no parametric tool.

## Description
Montessori teachers, homeschoolers, and Etsy material makers producing:
sandpaper-letter templates, movable-alphabet pieces, fraction circles/
skittles, geometry cabinet insets, number rods, printable three-part cards.
The DIY culture is large (900+-idea Pinterest printable boards; 10k+ laser
models indexed; 58+ free laser templates on file-library sites). Materials
follow AMI/AMS dimensional conventions — proportions are pedagogy.

## Problems Pathogen could address
Montessori materials are *specified* geometry: fraction insets must be
mathematically exact, geometry-cabinet shapes follow canonical dimension
sets, movable alphabets need consistent letterform sizing across the set.
Makers currently trace or copy files of varying fidelity. A spec-true
parametric library (with knob-hole placement, tray-fit tolerances) raises
the floor for the whole DIY ecosystem — and grading a set to a different
tray size is a parameter, not a redraw.

## Commercial value
Etsy material sellers (active market), homeschool curriculum bundles,
school-shop licensing. Modest; strategic overlap with D4's teachers-make-
users channel.

## Missing features
### Domain-specific [D]
- Spec libraries for canonical materials (fraction sets, insets, rods) with
  pedagogical dimension conventions
- Knob/tray-fit tolerance handling (material thickness again)
- Three-part-card layout kit (image + label + control cards)
### General [G]
- Physical units; modules (the spec library IS the value); multi-page PDF
  for card sets

## Project ideas (as of 2026-10-07)
Tiered by Pathogen skill and readiness. Web-researched; a dated snapshot.

### Beginner — buildable today
- **Printable fraction circles** — The standard home-made version of the
  fraction insets: ten circles divided from a whole into tenths, printed on
  red cardstock and laminated. Each circle is a loop of exact sectors, so a
  seventh is truly a seventh rather than an approximation dragged out in a
  drawing app. Text-to-path adds the fraction labels, and one PDF page per
  circle is ready to cut.
- **Constructive triangle sheets** — The constructive triangles are coloured
  triangles with black guide edges that children join into rectangles, rhombi
  and hexagons. Printable versions are among the most shared DIY Montessori
  files. Every triangle is defined by exact angles and side ratios, the guide
  edges are thick strokes on chosen sides, and layers keep the colours apart
  for printing on different card.
- **Inset shape design sheets** — The ten metal-inset shapes, from the circle
  and ellipse to the quatrefoil and curvilinear triangle, are widely printed
  as tracing and design sheets for pencil-control work. Drawing them from
  their constructions gives true curves, and the quatrefoil and curvilinear
  triangle fall out of boolean operations on circles. One file yields all ten
  outlines at a consistent size.

### Intermediate — several features, or one gap
- **Hundred board and tiles** — The hundred board is a ten-by-ten gridded
  board with a hundred loose numbered tiles and a printed control chart. Grid
  builds the board, a loop sets the numerals with text-to-path, and layers
  separate the board, the tiles and the control chart so each prints or cuts
  on its own. It brings several shipped features together and is the first
  material with parts that must match.
- **Number-rod strips** — The red-and-blue number rods step from one unit to
  ten in alternating colour bands, and paper versions are a staple printable.
  The geometry is trivial, but the pedagogical point is that each unit is
  exactly ten centimetres on the full-size set. Physical units [G] is the
  single gap: today the true size depends on print-scale settings that
  Pathogen cannot assert.
- **Geometry nomenclature cards** — Three-part cards for the geometric shapes
  need no photographs: each card shows a drawn shape and its name, in picture,
  label and control versions. One sheet is easy now. A complete set covering
  triangles, quadrilaterals and polygons runs to dozens of sheets, which meets
  the one gap, multi-page PDF [G], and until then means one export per sheet.

### Advanced — depends on a named gap
- **Laser-cut inset frames with knobs** — Makers on Etsy cut the metal insets
  in plywood: a square frame, a shape that lifts out, and a hole for a small
  knob. The inset must drop in freely without rattling, which depends on kerf
  and stock thickness. Knob and tray-fit tolerance handling [D] is the gap,
  and solving it serves every inset-style material in the catalogue.
- **Geometric cabinet drawer set** — The geometric cabinet holds six drawers
  of framed shapes whose sizes follow conventions teachers expect, such as the
  ten-centimetre inset square and the graded series of circles. Building one
  drawer by hand from a supplier's catalogue is feasible; building all six
  correctly is research. Spec libraries for canonical materials [D] is the
  dependency, and the profile notes that library is the value.
- **Botany and zoology three-part cards** — The most-sold Montessori
  printables are classified card sets, such as parts of a leaf or types of
  bird, with a picture card, a label and a control card for each term. The
  picture is an image, and the three versions must stay aligned across
  hundreds of cards. The three-part-card layout kit [D] is the gap that would
  open this market.

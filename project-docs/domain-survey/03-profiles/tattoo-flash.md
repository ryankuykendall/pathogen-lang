# Tattoo Flash & Stencils

**Tier:** physical-output · **Rubric:** Pop 3 · Pain 2 · Fit 3 · GapCost 3 · Adopters 2 = **108** · Longlist C9

## Snapshot
Flash sheets and thermal stencils are linework products with strict
transfer constraints — and 2026's trending styles (fine-line geometric,
cybersigilism, angel numbers) are unusually generative-geometry-shaped.

## Description
Tattoo artists drawing flash (pre-designed sheets clients pick from) in
Procreate, outputting thermal-printer stencils (Cricut-adjacent S640
thermocopiers). Linework must survive transfer and skin: minimum line
weights, spacing that won't blur over years. 2026 trends: Y2K revival
(fine-line barbed wire), cybersigilism (spiky abstract sigil geometry —
essentially generative), angel numbers, oversized blackwork.

## Problems Pathogen could address
The geometric substyles are parametric: sigil-like radial spikes, ornamental
mandala work, pattern-based sleeves (repeating motifs flowed along limb
curves — path-projection territory). Line-weight validation for stencil
transfer is a compile check. Flash-sheet layout (N designs + labels on a
sheet) is composition bookkeeping. Most flash, though, is figurative hand
drawing where Pathogen adds nothing — hence Fit 3.

## Commercial value
Flash sales between artists (flash-sharing marketplaces exist), stencil
prep services; modest and style-dependent.

## Missing features

## Project ideas (as of 2026-10-07)
Tiered by Pathogen skill and readiness. Web-researched; a dated snapshot.

### Beginner — buildable today
- **Fine-line mandala** — Mandalas are the perennial geometric request: one
  wedge of petals and arcs repeated around a centre. In Pathogen the wedge is
  a short path, the repeat count is a parameter, and a single stroke width
  keeps the linework uniform for the stencil. Changing eight petals to twelve
  regenerates the whole design, which makes a quick set of sibling pieces for
  a flash sheet.
- **Sacred-geometry minis** — Flower of life, seed of life and Metatron's cube
  are small, popular walk-in designs built entirely from circles and the lines
  joining their centres. They are exact constructions, so code draws them more
  accurately than a hand with a compass tool in Procreate. Each is a loop over
  angles, and the set shares one radius parameter so all the pieces scale
  together.
- **Moon-phase strip** — A row of moon phases, often run down a spine or
  across a collarbone, is a fine-line staple. Each crescent is one circle
  subtracted from another, so boolean operations produce clean closed outlines
  rather than overlapping strokes that would confuse a stencil. The number of
  phases and their spacing are parameters, and the same program gives a
  horizontal or vertical arrangement.

### Intermediate — several features, or one gap
- **Dotwork-shaded mandala** — Dotwork replaces solid shading with stippled
  dots that thin out toward the edge of a shape. A Grid or ring of candidate
  positions, thinned with the deterministic hash and clipped to each petal,
  gives stipple that is identical on every regeneration. That repeatability
  matters when a client approves a design: the stencil printed on the day
  matches the approved sheet dot for dot.
- **Angel-number lettering** — Repeating numerals such as 111 or 444 in a
  spiky or gothic face are among the most requested small tattoos.
  Text-to-path supplies the digits, and offsetting them gives the outline-only
  version most artists stencil. The one gap is robust text and ornament
  outlines [G]: sharp display faces are exactly where offset outlines tend to
  misbehave, so results need checking by eye.
- **Ornamental sternum piece** — Ornamental work is mirrored about the body's
  centre line and built from hanging arcs, beads and filigree drops. Drawing
  one half and mirroring it guarantees the symmetry clients notice first,
  repeated bead chains follow a curve by sampling it, and layers separate the
  main lines from the fine detail. It draws on several shipped features
  together but depends on none that is missing.

### Advanced — depends on a named gap
- **Seamless geometric armband** — Bands that circle the forearm must repeat
  cleanly where the ends meet and still look even on a limb that tapers toward
  the wrist. A flat strip is easy to generate; knowing how it will sit on the
  arm is not. Limb-wrap projection preview [D], flowing the pattern over a
  cylinder approximation, is the gap and would spare artists repeated paper
  test wraps.
- **Healed-size ladder** — Artists offer the same design at several sizes, and
  the small ones fail first as lines spread under the skin over the years. A
  sheet showing one design at three sizes, with each flagged where gaps fall
  below a safe minimum, would answer the client's usual question. Line-weight
  and spacing validation for transfer and aging [D] is the feature this
  depends on.
- **Placement mock-up over a client photo** — Before stencilling, many artists
  overlay the design on a photograph of the client's arm or back to agree size
  and position. Pathogen can generate and scale the ornament but cannot bring
  the photograph in. Image import for tracing references [G] is the gap; with
  it the geometric frame could be fitted around a traced reference instead of
  in a separate app.

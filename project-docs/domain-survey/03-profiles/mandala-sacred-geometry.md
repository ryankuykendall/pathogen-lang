# Mandala & Sacred Geometry Art

**Tier:** physical-output · **Rubric:** Pop 3 · Pain 2 · Fit 5 · GapCost 4 · Adopters 2 = **240** · Longlist C5

## Snapshot
Radial symmetric pattern art with a large mindfulness-framed audience —
low workflow pain, but near-perfect Pathogen fit and a direct feeder into
three other profiled domains (coloring books, stencils, laser decor).

## Description
Artists drawing mandalas (dot mandalas, line mandalas, Zentangle-adjacent
work) for wall art, painted rocks, laser-cut decor, and coloring pages;
sacred-geometry designers producing flower-of-life/metatron motifs for
prints and jewelry. Sold as finished art, templates, stencils, and coloring
content. Tools: compass and pencil, Procreate radial-symmetry brushes,
Illustrator rotate-repeat.

## Problems Pathogen could address
Radial repetition with per-ring parameter progression is the entire craft's
geometry — rings of petals/dots whose counts, sizes, and palettes step
systematically. Procreate gives live symmetry but no parameters; Illustrator
gives precision but no exploration. Dot-mandala *templates* (guide circles +
dot position grids) and stencil derivatives are direct outputs. Low pain
(artists enjoy the process) — Pathogen serves the template/product side, not
the meditative drawing itself.

## Commercial value
Feeds sibling markets: coloring interiors (C2), stencil products (A3),
laser-cut wall decor (A6 adjacency), template/workshop sales. As a hub motif
domain, its value is multiplied through them.

## Project ideas (as of 2026-10-07)
Tiered by Pathogen skill and readiness. Web-researched; a dated snapshot.

### Beginner — buildable today
- **Flower of Life print** — The most widely reproduced sacred-geometry
  figure: nineteen overlapping circles on a hexagonal arrangement inside a
  bounding ring. It is one circle placed by repeated rotation, clipped to the
  outer ring, with a colour harmony applied to the petals. A short program
  that produces a print-quality result is a persuasive first encounter for
  this audience.
- **Line-mandala colouring page** — A black-outline mandala for colouring:
  concentric rings of petals, arcs and dots, each ring a motif rotated around
  the centre. Writing each ring as one rotated shape and stacking five or six
  rings gives a finished page. Determinism means the page reproduces exactly,
  which matters to anyone selling colouring content.
- **Metatron's Cube** — Thirteen equal circles with every centre joined to
  every other by straight lines, popular for prints, tattoos and jewellery.
  The circle positions come from two rotated hexagon rings and the lines from
  a loop over pairs. It introduces computed construction, in place of ruler
  and compass, on a figure the audience already knows.

### Intermediate — several features, or one gap
- **Layered laser-cut mandala** — Multilayer mandala files are steady
  best-sellers for laser and paper cutters: five to eight stacked sheets, each
  with larger openings than the one beneath. Each layer is the same rotational
  design with cut-outs inset by a different amount, kept on its own layer.
  Rotation, offsets and booleans combine, and layer count becomes a parameter.
- **Sri Yantra construction** — Nine interlocking triangles whose
  intersections must coincide exactly; drawing one accurately by hand is
  famously difficult. Computing the triangle vertices so the triple crossings
  meet, then adding the lotus rings and gated square by rotation, combines
  intersection geometry with radial repetition. An exact figure is something
  this community recognises and values.
- **Colour-progression print series** — A series of mandala prints where hue
  and lightness step outward ring by ring, sold as a matching set. Colour
  harmonies and rotational structure are shipped, and each print is a
  parameter choice. Finding good choices is trial and error without live
  controls; playground parameter sliders are the one general gap this project
  meets.

### Advanced — depends on a named gap
- **Dot-mandala coaster template** — Dot painters work on coasters and rocks,
  and beginner kits include a practice sheet of guide circles and dot
  positions. Placing dots is easy; choosing diameters that grow ring by ring
  so neighbours just touch is the craft. A proper template product depends on
  the dot-mandala template kit listed as missing: guide circles plus a dot
  diameter ladder.
- **Mirror-symmetric rosette** — Many traditional rosettes, and the
  kaleidoscope style common on Instagram, have mirror lines as well as
  rotation, so each wedge is itself symmetric. Rotation alone forces the
  designer to mirror by hand and risks mismatched seams. It depends on the
  symmetry-group utilities beyond simple rotation that the profile lists,
  namely reflection groups.
- **Twelve-ring showpiece mandala** — Large commission pieces carry a dozen or
  more rings, with petal counts, sizes and rotations stepping in a deliberate
  sequence. Written ring by ring, the program becomes a long list of numbers
  that is hard to adjust. It depends on the ring and petal progression helpers
  listed as missing — count, size and rotation series per ring.

# Plush & Fursuit Patterns

**Tier:** physical-output · **Rubric:** Pop 2 · Pain 4 · Fit 3 · GapCost 2 · Adopters 3 = **144** · Longlist B6

## Snapshot
Plushie designers turn 2D character art into fabric panel patterns — a
passionate niche where dedicated 3D→pattern tools (Plushify) already exist,
confirming demand for computed patterns, but where the 3D-unwrap core is
beyond Pathogen's 2D scope.

## Description
Indie plush designers (Etsy/DeviantArt/convention sellers) and fursuit makers
drafting darted, gusseted panels for sewn character goods. The 2026 market
runs on personalization (embroidered names, custom pet replicas, QR story
cards) with adult buyers demanding high finish. Pattern design is iterative
prototyping: draft → sew muslin → adjust.

## Problems Pathogen could address
The 2D share of the work: pattern grading (one design, three plush sizes),
seam allowances (the recurring offset() story), mirrored pairs, dart/gusset
notation conventions, layout onto fabric widths with nap-direction arrows.
The genuinely hard core — deriving panels from a 3D form — is out of scope,
which caps Fit at 3; Pathogen serves the designers who already draft flat.

## Commercial value
Pattern sales (Etsy/Gumroad, active), commission work, kit sellers. Niche
but devoted; fursuit commissions are high-ticket.

## Missing features
### Domain-specific [D]
- Dart/gusset notation kit (fold lines, match points, notches)
- Size grading for plush proportions (non-uniform scaling rules)
- Fabric layout with nap/stretch direction arrows
### General [G]
- Reliable curve offset() for seam allowances (the garment blocker again);
  physical units; multi-page tiled PDF

## Project ideas (as of 2026-10-07)
Tiered by Pathogen skill and readiness. Web-researched; a dated snapshot.

### Beginner — buildable today
- **Two-piece flat plush** — The first project in most beginner plush guides,
  including the free starter packs: a front and a back cut from one outline,
  sewn around the edge and stuffed. The outline is drawn as a half and
  mirrored, labelled, and printed at exact scale as a stitch line the maker
  traces and sews along. No allowance geometry is needed.
- **Felt keychain plush with appliqué face** — A palm-sized felt character
  with separate eye, cheek and muzzle pieces stitched on top. Each felt colour
  gets its own group of small labelled shapes, and the face pieces are
  mirrored so the two sides match. Felt does not fray and is cut on the line,
  which keeps the pattern within flat drafting and labels.
- **Ear and tail accessory set** — Cat or fox ears on a headband and a simple
  tail — a common first convention project and a small fursuit-adjacent seller
  item. Inner and outer ear pieces are nested shapes, drawn once and mirrored
  for the pair, with labels for fabric and quantity. It prints on one or two
  pages and teaches paired-piece conventions.

### Intermediate — several features, or one gap
- **Ball plush from gores** — The round body behind countless kawaii animals
  is a sphere sewn from four to six identical pointed gores. The gore curve
  can be computed from the finished diameter, so size becomes a parameter.
  Adding a sewing allowance around that curved outline is where the unreliable
  curve offset bites — the one gap, and the reason the stitch-line version
  stays the safe output.
- **Long snake or bolster plush** — A metre-long snake, dragon body or body
  pillow: simple shapes, but far larger than a sheet of paper. The pieces are
  easy to draft and mirror. Printing them at home means splitting across pages
  with marks to tape them together, and the multi-page tiled PDF is listed as
  a general gap, so today each page is set up separately.
- **Embroidery placement guide** — Plush faces are usually embroidered before
  sewing, and patterns include a guide showing eye, nose and mouth positions
  on the face panel. The guide is the panel outline plus mirrored feature
  shapes and centre lines on a second layer, with labels. It combines layers,
  mirroring and text into the sheet buyers ask for most.

### Advanced — depends on a named gap
- **Darted chibi-head plush** — A large rounded head shaped by darts at the
  crown and chin, the construction behind most character and human-style
  plush. The pattern needs dart legs, fold lines and match points drawn
  consistently so pieces align when sewn. That depends on the missing dart and
  gusset notation kit, the conventions buyers rely on to read a pattern.
- **Fursuit hand paws fitted to the wearer** — Padded hand paws are the usual
  first fursuit part, and they must fit a particular hand: finger length and
  palm width change independently. Scaling the whole pattern uniformly gives
  wrong fingers. This depends on size grading with non-uniform rules, listed
  as missing, and is the high-ticket commission end of the domain.
- **Faux-fur bodysuit cutting layout** — A fursuit bodysuit uses several
  metres of long-pile fur whose nap must run the same way on every piece, with
  markings split across colours. Makers plan the cutting layout on the floor.
  Arranging pieces on a fabric width with nap and stretch arrows is the
  missing fabric layout feature, and mistakes here cost expensive fur.

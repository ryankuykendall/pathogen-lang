# Foam Armor & Cosplay Patterns

**Tier:** physical-output · **Rubric:** Pop 3 · Pain 4 · Fit 3 · GapCost 2 · Adopters 3 = **216** · Longlist A18

## Snapshot
EVA-foam armor is built from flat pattern pieces (helmets, breastplates,
bracers) sold as PDF+SVG/DXF packs — with the domain's core chore being
scaling patterns to a specific body, piece by piece.

## Description
Cosplayers and prop makers heat-shaping EVA foam from patterns. The pattern
economy (Etsy, Gumroad — Pretzl Cosplay tier sellers) ships PDF print packs
plus SVG/DXF for cutting machines, with part labels, build order, and
registration marks. Design happens in Illustrator or via 3D-model unwrapping
(Armorsmith is the domain's dedicated scaling tool). Community lives on
TikTok/YouTube build content.

## Problems Pathogen could address
Patterns are static sizes; fitting means scaling *per body measurement* — and
naive uniform scaling breaks (a bracer scales with wrist girth but not length
equally). Parametric patterns driven by a measurement table are the garment
post's thesis in foam. Part labelling, mirrored left/right pairs,
registration marks, and bevel-angle annotations (45° edges for joins) map to
labels and line classes. The 3D-unwrap ceiling is real (complex curved
armor), but a large share of pieces are developable flats.

## Commercial value
Active pattern-seller economy with multi-format packs already standard;
measurement-driven "fits you" patterns would be a visible tier upgrade;
convention workshop content.

## Missing features
### Domain-specific [D]
- Measurement-table-driven grading (wrist/forearm/bicep girths → bracer)
- Bevel-angle line classes (45°/90° edge annotations for foam joins)
- Mirrored pair + part-label kit with build-order numbering
- Multi-page tiled PDF at 100% with registration (shared need)
### General [G]
- Data import (measurement tables); physical units; modules (armor part
  libraries)

## User base
Cosplay is millions strong (r/cosplay ~1M+); foam-armor builders est.
200k–500k · confidence **L, unverified**. Pattern sellers the adopter tier.

- **Where they gather:** TikTok/YouTube build channels (the discovery layer),
  r/cosplayers and prop-making Discords, Etsy/Gumroad pattern shops,
  convention maker tracks.
- **Talking about right now:** multi-format pattern packs (PDF + SVG/DXF +
  build notes) are the professionalized norm; beginner-to-intermediate
  positioning dominates listings; steady helmet/breastplate/bracer canon.
  (etsy.com markets, gumroad shops, youtube build playlists)
- **Obsessed with:** clean bevels and seams, heat-forming technique, scaling
  patterns to fit, budget builds.
- **Blog content angles:** (1) a bracer graded from three measurements — the
  "pattern that fits *you*" story; (2) bevel angles as labelled line classes;
  (3) one pattern → print PDF + Cricut SVG pack.

## Pathogen fit today
Flat pattern geometry, mirroring, labels, exact-scale PDF all work; the
measurement-driven core waits on data import (GapCost 2), and curved-armor
unwrapping is out of scope.

## Proposed validation project
A parametric bracer: three girth measurements in, labelled mirrored pattern
pair with bevel classes and build numbering out — foam-built to verify fit
against a straight-scaled version.

## Project ideas (as of 2026-10-07)
Tiered by Pathogen skill and readiness. Web-researched; a dated snapshot.

### Beginner — buildable today
- **Tasset and belt-plate set** — The hanging hip plates in every basic
  medieval armor pack: a few flat, gently tapered plates worn in mirrored left
  and right sets. Each plate is one outline drawn once and mirrored, with a
  part label and a strap-slot mark, printed on a single page at exact scale. A
  first pattern that needs nothing beyond flat geometry, mirroring and labels.
- **Layered foam emblem** — A chest badge or belt buckle built from stacked
  2mm craft foam — a base shape, a raised border and a crest on top, the
  detail trick taught in most beginner foam tutorials. Each foam thickness
  becomes a set of flat labelled pieces sharing one centre line, so the stack
  registers when glued. Small enough for one exact-scale page, and entirely
  flat pattern work.
- **Circlet or crown band** — A fantasy circlet: one long symmetric band with
  a peaked centre and tapering sides, heat-curved around the head after
  cutting. The pattern is a half-outline mirrored about the centre line,
  labelled with fold and centre marks, and printed at exact scale. It shows
  the pattern-as-program idea on a piece where symmetry is the whole design
  problem.

### Intermediate — several features, or one gap
- **Layered pauldron with lames** — The shoulder armor from the standard
  pattern collections: a domed cap plus three or four overlapping lames that
  step down the arm. The lames are one curve repeated at shrinking sizes, so a
  loop generates the family, mirrors it for the other shoulder and labels each
  plate in order. Several shipped features working together, with the dome
  itself still shaped by heat.
- **Foam scale-mail sheet** — Scale mail for skirts and sleeves: hundreds of
  identical leaf-shaped scales cut from thin foam and glued in offset rows. A
  repeated row layout produces the cut sheet and a second layer the placement
  guide, with scale size as one parameter. Sizing scales to a real waist in
  millimetres is where the physical-units gap shows; today the maker converts
  by hand.
- **Basic armor pack from one source** — The seller's staple product —
  bracers, greaves and tassets sold together as a print PDF plus an SVG for
  cutting machines. One program emits both formats with matching labels and
  mirrored pairs, which is the pack norm buyers expect. Sharing strap slots,
  label styles and edge details across the pieces is where the missing modules
  feature starts to pinch.

### Advanced — depends on a named gap
- **Full armor set graded to one wearer** — The complete helmet-to-sabaton
  collections sold in fixed male and female sizes, which every buyer rescales
  piece by piece. A set driven by one measurement table — chest, bicep, thigh,
  calf — would regrade every part consistently instead of uniformly. It
  depends on measurement-table-driven grading and on data import, the
  profile's central gap and Pathogen's clearest opening here.
- **Breastplate with bevel-coded seams** — A multi-panel chest plate whose
  curve comes from angled edge cuts: some seams bevelled at 45 degrees, others
  butted square, assembled in a fixed sequence. The pattern needs each edge
  marked with its cut angle and each part numbered in build order. That is the
  missing bevel-angle line classes and the part-label kit — annotation sellers
  now draw by hand.
- **Oversized sword or shield prop** — A greatsword or a Hylian-style shield,
  well beyond one sheet of paper, which builders print in tiles and tape
  together before tracing onto foam. Getting the tiles to align is the usual
  complaint. This needs the multi-page tiled PDF at 100% with registration
  marks listed as missing — a shared gap that also serves quilting, garment
  and plush patterns.
